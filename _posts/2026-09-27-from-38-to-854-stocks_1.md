*By Abhishek Kalhan, September 27, 2026*

## Where things stand

TrustLens predicts the direction of a stock from StockTwits posts, giving more weight to authors whose past calls were right more often. Last month I settled the trust formula: an author's trust is simply the share of their past calls that turned out right. This month was about testing it properly, and the answer changed before the month was over.

My goal for September was simple to say and hard to do: find out, on data the model had never seen, whether weighting people by their track record beats just averaging everybody. The issue was that my first honest answer was "no", and I had to work out whether that "no" was real.

## What I worked on this month

### Testing the main idea on 38 stocks

In early September I finished the walk-forward test. Walk-forward means the model trains on everything before a date, is tested on the next three months, and then the window slides forward and repeats. I ran 12 of these rounds on 38 stocks, with posts from 2018 to 2022.

The result was clear and disappointing. Trust weighting lost to the plain crowd in 12 out of 12 rounds at the 3-day horizon. I also found something I had never checked: the plain crowd on its own is not just weak, it points the wrong way. On busy days, when most people are bullish, the stock tends to go down.

### Copying what other papers did

At my 9 September check-in, Prof. Marpaung told me to look at how other researchers made trust weighting work and copy their methods. I read the papers again and turned them into a work list of 20 items. I replicated five published methods on my data (Bar-Haim et al. 2011, Bouadjenek et al. 2023, Zhou et al. 2025, Cookson et al. 2024 and Kakhbod et al. 2023). The pattern across all five was the same: when a method looked like it worked, it was mostly correcting a biased crowd, not finding skilled authors.

I also opened my held-back data from 2024 to 2026 for one final check. The strongest signal there had no posts in it at all. It was the market's own recent movement.

### Asking whether 38 stocks could even tell

This was the turning point. Instead of trying yet another trust formula, I asked a different question: if trust really helped by a small amount, could 38 stocks ever show it? I worked out the smallest difference 38 stocks could reliably detect. It was 0.014 in AUC. The difference I was actually seeing was 0.006. So my test could never have said "yes" even if the answer was yes. The math said I needed about 280 stocks.

AUC is the score I use throughout. 0.5 is a coin flip, higher is better, and below 0.5 means the signal points the wrong way.

So I expanded the test. I wrote down a fixed rule for picking stocks before looking at any results: at least 4,000 posts between 2018 and 2022, a clean price history, a price above one dollar, and no funds. 854 stocks passed. I test them as two separate groups: the **Busy 300**, the most talked-about stocks, and the **Quiet 554**, stocks with only 5 to 30 posts a day. That is 48.7 million labeled posts, kept in a separate database so my recorded 38-stock results could never change by accident.

The answer flipped. On the Busy 300, the trust spread beats the plain crowd at 1, 3 and 20 days ahead. The trust spread is how bullish the most reliable third of authors is, minus how bullish the least reliable third is. It won in 14 or 15 of the 16 test quarters at every horizon, and a bootstrap test over whole stocks gave p = 0.001, which means luck is very unlikely to explain it.

### Making sure the comparison was fair

My first write-up said trust added 3.9 accuracy points. Then I noticed the models with trust were only tested on days when trusted and untrusted authors were both posting, while the models without trust were tested on every day. That is not a fair race. So I wrote a "same-days check" that runs both versions on exactly the same days. With a fair comparison, trust still helps in 17 of 18 comparisons, and the model with trust goes from barely above a coin flip to clearly better at every horizon. I withdrew the 3.9 number.

![Same model, same days, with and without trust](/assets/images/trustlens-sameday-auc.png)

The check also caught something I had to be honest about. My best accuracy, 64.5%, mostly comes from picking which days to act on, not from trust, because the same model without trust gets 64.2% on those days.

### Profiling memory for the performance assignment

For the performance assignment I profiled memory across the pipeline with memory_profiler and a small sampler I wrote with psutil. The step that builds the daily trust signal peaked at 2.67 GB in early 2021, the meme-stock quarter. The line-by-line report showed one line was responsible: it loaded millions of posts with their tickers, dates and "Bullish"/"Bearish" words stored as separate Python strings. I changed the data types and read the posts in chunks. The peak dropped to 0.80 GB and the output stayed exactly the same.

![Signal build memory before and after the fix](/assets/images/trustlens-memory-before-after.png)

### Tools and resources

Python, pandas, SQLite, scikit-learn (logistic regression, linear SVM, random forest), LightGBM, memory_profiler, psutil, Git and GitHub, and Jira and Confluence for tracking. The papers above were my main references, along with Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen (2023) on how to check whether a finance result holds up when someone else tests it.

## Challenges

The hardest part was not technical. It was staying honest while the answer kept moving. After two weeks of "trust does not work", it would have been easy to either give up or grab the first good number. The same-days check was a good example: my own exciting number turned out to be partly an unfair comparison, and I had to take it back myself.

## Retrospective

### What went right this month

- I tested before I argued. Every claim in the project now has a script behind it that first reproduces a number I already recorded before I trust anything new it tells me.
- Asking "could my test even see this?" turned a dead end into the main result of the thesis.
- Prof. Marpaung approved the expansion on 23 September, agreed with the new headline (trust weighting works on active days, at scale, with 20 days as the main horizon), and asked for a live demo of a 2022 replay as the dashboard.

### What went wrong this month

- I should have done the power math in August, before spending weeks trying new trust formulas on a sample that was too small to judge them.
- I opened my held-back data earlier than my plan said, and I started the expansion runs before the plan change was approved. Both are reported openly as deviations, but I would rather not have needed to.
- I reported the 3.9-point result before checking that both models were tested on the same days.

### How I can improve moving forward

- When I get a null result, check first whether the test was big enough to see an effect at all.
- Put the fair comparison next to every claim before I share it, not after.
- Stop adding new tests. The development freeze is 30 September, and what is left is the dashboard and the writing.

## What I learned

The biggest lesson was that a "no" can mean two different things: "it does not work" or "I cannot tell yet". Statistics from earlier courses told me about sample size, but this was the first time it decided the outcome of my own project. Profiling taught a similar lesson in a smaller way: I expected model training to use the most memory, and it was actually one database query in a different script. You do not know where the problem is until you measure.

In terms of time, September was the busiest month so far. Most of it went into testing, and I used the weekly check-ins well: each meeting with Prof. Marpaung changed what I did the next week, from copying published methods on 9 September, to looking for better accuracy on 16 September, to approving the expansion on 23 September.

## Ready for the next phase

Almost. Testing is finished and the headline is agreed with my advisor. Next is building the dashboard for the 30 September freeze, then rewriting thesis chapters 1 to 4 around the new results by 11 October.
